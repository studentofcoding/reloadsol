Sizing · what PnL (sized) means

PnL (sized) is a volume knob on a nearly constant signal

Every SOL figure on the PnL dashboard is computed twice — once at a flat 0.005 SOL stake, once at the stake the sizing pipeline chose. This page is what sits between those two numbers. Read straight from strategy_outcomes and trading_records on production for 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01 (Asia/Bangkok). All of it is paper: every strategy is execution_mode = sim_only and there are zero real outcome rows.

It is one number, twice

  • ml_size_mult equals the closed-loop score cl_p — exact match on 748 of 748 rows.
  • The ML risk head it is documented to use (ml_p_bad) is stamped on 0 of 800 rows. It never runs.

The −65% is arithmetic

  • It is not a risk decision. Mean multiplier 0.356 on winners, 0.359 on losers.
  • Per SOL staked the two are the same edge: +69.7% flat, +68.1% sized.

The two-sided knob: rejected

  • Modelled on 646 rows, the normalized tilt returns 1.90 SOL vs flat's 2.25 — token-clustered t = −2.5.
  • The one scheme that beat flat is the fail-soft guard: +2.30 SOL at 75.3% ROI, one line.

Where the multiplier comes from

One helper, one call site, one line that decides everything:

// src/strategies/ml-soft-size.ts — softMlSize()
mult = Math.max(SOL_ML_SIZE_FLOOR, (1 - pBad) * confidence)   // floor defaults 0.25

// src/strategies/target-machine-cl-size.ts — sizeFromClosedLoop()
softMlSize(baseSol, { pBad: 1 - p })                          // so (1 - pBad) collapses back to p
// → mult = max(0.25, cl_p), and cl_p is stamped as cl_p on features.domain_features

Because cl_p ≤ 1 and the expression is max(floor, cl_p), the function can only ever be a haircut — its ceiling is the flat stake. It is structurally incapable of sizing up. And ml_p_bad, the risk head the docstring describes, is written by neither path: stampTargetMachineCl calls stampMlSize(features, sized) with no pBad extra.

That is the premise the rest of this page now builds on — and it is why the model below rejects the two-sided knob rather than shipping it.

Modelled: what Levels 1 and 2 would have returned

The formulas, then a backtest on the only window that can carry one. The cl_p spine is four days old — it first appears on 09-25 and only reaches full coverage on 09-30 — so the testable sample is the same two days as the report at the top of this page: 646 trades, ~98% of them scored. Every scheme below is scored on those identical rows at the same 0.005 SOL base, so the only thing that differs between the bars is how the stake was weighted.

Level 2 — normalized tilt (the candidate)
  q_s  = within-strategy percentile rank of cl_p        // 0..1
  mult = 0.5 + q_s                                      // in [0.5, 1.5], mean about 1
         1.0 where cl_p is missing or the 0.5 fail-soft

Fail-soft guard (today's accident, made deliberate)
  mult = 0.25 if cl_p IS NULL or cl_p = 0.5 else 1.0

Level 1 — market scalar
  mult = clamp(1 - beta * marketRisk, 0.25, 1.0)        // one number, applied to the whole book

Realistic composition
  stake = SIM_BASE_POSITION_SOL * marketScalar * multFailSoft

Level 2 is rejected, not deferred. The tilt's excess over flat is −10.7 points per trade — t = −4.54 if trades were independent. They are not (the same token is traded many times), so cluster by token and it stays negative and significant: 332 clusters, t = −2.52. It does not fail for want of power; it fails because the thing it tilts on has no rank power to amplify.

Level 1 needs no model to state its expectation: it is exactly linear. A uniform scalar multiplies every trade, so a 0.7 scalar returns 0.7 × the flat PnL and 0.7 × the drawdown — there is nothing to fit, and no backtest can tell you anything a multiplication cannot. That is why it can ship as a plain risk dial the moment someone names the signal that drives it. Today no such signal exists: market_regime_tags stopped in July and appears on no recent close.

And what does pay is smaller than a knob. D — size the unscored trades down to the 0.25 floor — beats flat on both PnL and ROI, and it is one line needing no new model. E — stop trading four strategies that lose regardless of size — earns more PnL from 75 fewer trades. The two are the same finding seen twice: every missing-model row lives in the strategies E drops. Those trades should not be taken at all, so the correct stake for them is not a small one — it is zero.

What this means

  • PnL (sized) is not a risk-adjusted number — it is PnL (flat) times the average multiplier. With the multiplier at ~0.356 on winners and ~0.359 on losers, the sized line is the flat line at one-third size: +68.1% per SOL staked against +69.7%. The sizingEffectPct of −65% is arithmetic, not a finding.
  • It cannot maximize profit, by construction. mult = max(0.25, cl_p) with cl_p ∈ [0,1] means the ceiling is the flat stake. There is no branch that sizes up a high-conviction trade — so the knob is a volume control wearing a risk control's name.
  • And it does not minimize loss either, because the score has no rank power at all. Not within a strategy (the two mass tiers are +87.0 on n=73 against +96.6 on n=88, t ≈ 0.25) and not across them (gmgn sits at 0.389–0.422 with a 12–13% win rate while the 55%-win mcap family sits at 0.341–0.389). What looked like a within-strategy gradient was four trades. Fed into any multiplier — today's or the proposed two-sided one — an unranked score is just noise applied to the stake.
  • The two-sided knob is therefore rejected, not deferred. Modelled on all 646 rows of the only fully-covered window, the normalized tilt returns +1.90 SOL against flat's +2.25, at 67.5% ROI against 69.5% — and it stays significantly negative after clustering by token (t = −2.52, 332 clusters) rather than the t = −4.54 you get by pretending the trades are independent. It does not fail for wanting power. It fails because it amplifies a signal that is not there. Building it shadow-first would have been the right process for a promising candidate; this one is not one.
  • The one scheme that beat flat is the fail-soft guard, and it is one line. A missing model falls soft to cl_p = 0.5 — today the largest multiplier in the book — and those 37 rows win 16.2% (15 of social's 26 rows; zero in every mcap family). Sizing them down to the 0.25 floor returns +2.30 SOL at 75.3% ROI, beating flat on both. Note the direction: the fix is to size absence of evidence down, not to make it neutral — moving 0.5 → 1.0 makes results worse, not better.
  • The biggest lever is not sizing at all. Dropping the four strategies that lose regardless of stake returns +2.39 SOL at 83.6% ROI on 571 trades — more PnL from 75 fewer trades. And the guard and the selection turn out to be one finding seen twice: every missing-model row lives in the strategies the drop removes. Those trades should not be taken at all, so the correct stake is not a small one, it is zero.
  • Level 1 costs nothing to state and nothing to build, because it is linear. A uniform market scalar returns exactly its own value times the flat PnL and the drawdown — a 0.7 scalar is a 30% haircut on both. There is no model to fit; it ships as a risk dial the moment a signal is named. Today none is: market_regime_tags stopped in July and is on no recent close.
  • Sizing is a rounding error next to selection, and that is the honest summary of this page. Every sizing scheme lands inside a 0.5 SOL band over two days; choosing which strategies to run is worth an order of magnitude more, and it needs no new model, no shadow period and no risk headroom. And the risk-cap question stays open regardless: prod carries MAX_SOL_AT_RISK=0.1 against a paper peak of 0.42 SOL, so anything that raises the average stake from today's 0.38 has to be paired with a lower base.
