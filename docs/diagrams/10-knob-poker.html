Sizing, round two · the poker view

Two knobs, two logics, one verdict

Level 1 shrinks the whole book when the market is dangerous. Level 2 weighs each trade by how much the model likes it. This page tests both two ways — first by backtest, then by poker arithmetic, which is an independent route because it never looks at the strategy data at all. Both point the same way, and the combination that actually wins is neither of them. Spine era (2026-09-30 → 10-01, 651 closes, base 0.005 SOL), all paper.

Level 1 is the right layer

  • A market scalar is bankroll management — the correct poker layer.
  • Its effect is exactly linear, so there is nothing to fit and nothing to shadow.

Level 2 is the wrong layer

  • It sizes up on a tell that does not discriminate — no rank power within a strategy or across them.
  • Combined with what works, it destroys value: 2.026 vs 2.383 SOL.

What wins instead

  • Flat × fold × tighter stop = 2.524 SOL at 87.6% ROI, best on both measures.
  • The stop was the one axis never swept.

Second test: poker arithmetic, which never sees the data

The arithmetic that follows

  • The edge is the odds, not the read. Break-even at 15.5% against an actual 47.2% means the profit comes from what you get paid when you hit, not from hitting more often. Every hour spent on entry modelling is an hour spent trying to raise a number that is already 3× its requirement.
  • With no card information, the optimal bet is a flat fraction of bankroll. That is fractional Kelly, and it is what the grid independently found: flat beats every weighting scheme. The two tests agree because they are the same statement — you cannot size a hand you cannot read.
  • The stop is the axis you never swept. Take-profit was swept three ways (142 / 189 / 283) and the win rate did not move (56.7 / 55.2 / 57.1). The stop has sat at −31.7 the entire time. Tightening it is worth more than selection on PnL and more than anything Level 2 could ever have offered.
  • 183 of 651 closes fell below −16%. Clamping them there lifts the total from 44,866 to 49,765 percentage-points of PnL — a +10.9% improvement from the loss leg alone. This is an upper bound: it ignores that some trades which dipped past −16 went on to win, so the true figure is lower. It still directionally beats every entry-side idea on this page.

And if we combine it?

The question has a precise answer, and it is not "Level 1 × Level 2". Sections 1–4 of the grid are the evidence: adding Level 2 to a working combination makes it worse (2.383 → 2.026 SOL, 82.8% → 81.9% ROI). A knob with no edge does not average out when you multiply it into one that has an edge — it just adds variance and takes a cut.

The combination that does work replaces Level 2 with something that is not a weighting: a binary fold. Poker's version of "size by conviction" is to fold the hand, because you cannot read the cards but you can choose the game. A fold is mult = 0 — the one weight the current formula cannot express, since its floor is 0.25.

What we have today
  stake = 0.005 * max(0.25, cl_p)                        # a read that reads nothing

What wins on both measures
  stake = SIM_BASE_POSITION_SOL * marketScalar           # Level 1: bankroll, equal for every trade
        * (strategySelectable ? 1 : 0)                   # Level 2 as a fold, not a tilt
        ; stopLoss = -16                                 # the axis never swept

Measured, 651 closes, 0.005 SOL base
  today                              +0.786 SOL    63.6% ROI   (651 trades)
  flat, fold 4, stop -16             +2.524 SOL    87.6% ROI   (576 trades)

Selection earns more than sizing ever did, and it is binary. Note what the fold actually is: three of the nine "strategies" are the same hand played three ways — the TP variants share 66 mints across all three and 87 across two of them, so their 433 trades are really 238 distinct hands. Nine strategies is four games, and three of the nine are the same game at three exit settings that do not matter.

Ranked, by poker logic

  1. Delete the multiplier. No card information means no per-hand sizing. Flat fractional Kelly, and the grid says flat wins.
  2. Sweep the stop, not the take-profit. The only unexplored axis, and the one your odds live on: R from 5.5 to 13.0, break-even from 15.5% to 7.2%.
  3. Fold the three losing families. Selection is binary and it is worth more than any weighting — and it removes the fail-soft rows with it, so the guard becomes moot.
  4. Promote the shadowed exit models. The strategies are preflop-only: everything is decided at entry and never revisited, while ml_exit_overlay and gmgn_exit_boost sit wired-but-inert. That is playing the turn and river, and it is already built.
  5. Level 1 is not "ship when a signal exists" — it is shipped, and it needed reach, not a driver. The brain is live (ok: true, stale: false, h = 0.576, headline "BTC is dumping — beware") and the scalar is a five-tier lookup, not a curve: it reads a constant 0.25 because a cascade veto clamps it to trim, which is correct behaviour for a sustained dump. The gap was that only mcap/search resolved it. That is now fixed for every domain — so a strategy re-tuned later is already measured on the same risk footing as its neighbours.

What this means

  • Two independent tests, one verdict. The backtest says the tilt loses (t = −2.5 clustered) and the poker arithmetic says a per-trade weight cannot help when there is no read. They agree because they are describing the same absence from two directions — one empirical, one theoretical.
  • Level 1 is not a knob you have to build; it is a knob you have to drive. Being exactly linear is a feature: a 0.7 scalar is a 30% haircut on both PnL and drawdown, no fitting required. The open question is the signal, not the mechanism.
  • Combining them is strictly worse than either alone. Level 1 × Level 2 inherits Level 2's deficit and Level 1's absent signal. The productive combination is a different decomposition: bankroll (level) × selection (binary) × stop (payoff ratio).
  • The honest caveat on the stop number. +10.9% is an upper bound computed by clamping realised losses, not by re-simulating price paths. A real sweep has to replay the candles, because a trade that dipped to −18% and recovered to +300% would be wrongly counted as stopped. The direction is solid; the magnitude is not yet.
  • And the sample is two days. 651 closes, one market regime, all paper. Every number on this page is a snapshot of an open window and moves as closes land — which is also why the row counts differ by a handful from 09-pnl-sizing.html.
